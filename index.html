The gap

The flagship has been a locked door — and it's on the record.

DJ software is built to be seen: waveforms, the library, the cues, which track is on which deck. A screen reader gets almost none of it. We measured it.

2 / 124
Of Serato's entire interface, a screen reader can read two elements out of a hundred and twenty-four.

So a blind DJ has three bad choices: pay a sighted person to sit next to them all night, memorize everything and mix blind, or give up on the pro tools. Talent was never the barrier. Access is.

The insight

You don't make the screen readable. You get the facts out — and let the controller drive.

AccessBridge does three things, and it never claims a value it did not actually read.

Reads the software from the inside

It reads Serato's live internal state directly — the track on each deck, time left, key, BPM, cues, effects, crates — read-only, no screen capture, no fake clicks. djay it reads through the Mac's own accessibility system. Nothing guessed.

Names the hardware under your hands

Plug in a controller and every knob, pad and fader announces what it does the instant you touch it — with zero setup, in both hosts. A blind DJ could never map a controller by sight; AccessBridge ships that knowledge built in.

Speaks — and drives

One clean voice, through the screen reader the DJ already trusts. And it drives back: load a track, set a cue, search and sort the library, jump a beat — all from the keyboard or the controller.

Two opposite problems. Two doors in. One seamless experience.

Serato Read from the inside

Serato is built to be seen — only 2 of its 124 interface elements reach a screen reader, and there is no official way in. So a small component rides safely inside Serato and reads its real, live objects directly: read-only, no screen capture, no synthetic clicks.

djay Pro Read the honest way

djay is the reverse — already properly accessible, publishing its decks and library to the Mac's accessibility system. So AccessBridge doesn't re-read what djay says well. It does the one thing djay can't: name the controller under the DJ's hands.

The market & the moment

Overdue, and right on time.

Two forces make this the moment

  • Accessibility is becoming law, not goodwill. The EU Accessibility Act takes effect in 2025 and ADA digital-enforcement keeps expanding. Shipped access is compliance and reputation insurance.
  • Scale. The WHO puts vision impairment at 2.2 billion people; tens of millions are blind. The blind DJ community is real, vocal, and has been asking for exactly this for years.
  • A concrete wedge. Be the default accessibility layer for the two dominant DJ platforms — championed by the community's own artists — with a natural path to licensing with the platform vendors themselves.

Built for the blind. Better for everyone.

Accessibility rarely stays niche. Captions, voice control and screen readers all started as "for a few" and became "for everyone." The same engine that names a controller by ear is a powerful hands-free layer for any DJ — eyes on the crowd, not the laptop.
