AccessBridge — the proof.

The #1 DJ platform was built to be seen. A screen reader reads 2 of its 124 interface elements. AccessBridge lets a blind DJ run Serato DJ Pro and djay Pro entirely by ear — no sighted help, nothing to set up. Working software, in testers' hands now.

Reads from the inside

It reads Serato's live internal state directly — decks, time, key, BPM, cues, effects, crates — read-only, no screen capture, no fake clicks. Nothing guessed.

Names the hardware

Every knob, pad and fader announces itself the instant you touch it — zero setup, in both hosts. That knowledge ships built in.

Speaks, and drives

One clean voice through the screen reader the DJ trusts — and it drives back: load a track, set a cue, search the library, jump a beat.

Serato Read from the inside

Only 2 of 124 elements reach a screen reader and there's no official way in — so a small component rides safely inside Serato and reads its real, live objects. Read-only. No screen capture. No synthetic clicks.

djay Pro Read the honest way

The market & the moment

Overdue, and right on time.

Why now

  • Accessibility is becoming law. The EU Accessibility Act takes effect in 2025; ADA digital enforcement keeps expanding. Shipped access is compliance and reputation insurance.
  • Scale. The WHO puts vision impairment at 2.2 billion people; tens of millions are blind — a real, vocal community that has asked for this for years.
  • A repeatable wedge. Own the access layer for one flagship category, then carry the same brand and method to the next. AccessBridge is the template.

Built for the blind. Better for everyone.

The same engine that names a controller by ear is a powerful hands-free layer for any user — eyes on the work, not the screen. That's how accessibility businesses grow past their first market: the tech is universal, the empathy is the moat.
