02The launch product — AccessBridge

The proof the model works.

The number-one DJ platform was built to be seen — a screen reader reads two of its 124 controls. AccessBridge lets a blind DJ run Serato DJ Pro and djay Pro entirely by ear: no sighted help, nothing to set up. Working software, in testers’ hands today.

2 / 124
Interface elements a screen reader can read — now all of it, spoken by ear.
30,000+
Lines of Swift & C++ shipped, guarded by a 470+ automated-test suite.
1 in 10,000
A Serato planner, on priority: “if only 1 in 10,000 DJs need this, it makes it a lot harder to prioritise.”

i. Reads from the inside

It reads Serato’s live internal state directly — decks, time left, key, BPM, cues, effects, the crates — read-only, no screen capture, no fake clicks. djay it reads through the Mac’s accessibility system. Nothing is guessed.

ii. Names the hardware

Every knob, pad and fader announces itself the instant you touch it — zero setup, in both hosts, across 200-plus controllers. A blind DJ could never map a controller by sight; that knowledge ships built in.

iii. Speaks, and drives

One clean voice through the screen reader the DJ already trusts — and it drives back: load a track, set a cue, sort and search the library, jump a beat, from the keyboard or the controller.

04The market & the moment

Overdue, and right on time.

  • Accessibility is becoming law.The EU Accessibility Act takes effect in 2025, and ADA digital-accessibility enforcement keeps expanding. A shipped solution is compliance and reputation insurance.
  • The scale is real.The WHO puts vision impairment at 2.2 billion people; tens of millions are blind — a vocal community that has asked for exactly this for years.
  • A repeatable wedge.Own the access layer for one flagship category, then carry the same brand and method to the next. AccessBridge is the template, not the ceiling.

Built for the blind. Better for everyone.

The same engine that names a controller by ear is a powerful hands-free layer for any professional — eyes on the work, not the screen. That is how accessibility companies grow past their first market: the technology is universal, the empathy is the moat.
